# POV Water Slide — Examples

# Examples

Four complete builds. Same eight-beat grammar every time — **vertigo reveal → hesitation → commit + hop → first drop → mid-ride → second spectacle → final descent → splashdown** — with the route, the light and the spectacle rewritten by the environment.

Every one opens with the rider already at the edge and **completely still**, looking down. None contains a step, a stair or an approach — and none begins already in motion.

## Example A — Night city · thunderstorm · blue LED · hybrid · rooftop pool · 15 seconds

*The "Surprise me" default. The primary reference reproduced, minus its four-second walk-up.*

Answers: `1E, 2A, 3E, 4B, 5C, 6B, 7B`

# VIDEO PROMPT

```
Vertical 9:16, hyperrealistic single-take first-person POV. The rider is already standing completely still at the very edge of a wet black glass roof deck on a supertall skyscraper, bare legs and feet filling the lower third of the frame, the deck's edge crossing the middle of the shot and simply stopping. Just ahead and slightly below sits the flared mouth of an enormous transparent water slide, small in frame at this distance, with a sheet of water pouring over its lip and vanishing. Beyond that edge there is nothing but four hundred metres of open air and a vast night city, where avenues run to the horizon as thin unbroken chains of orange sodium light, traffic crawls as barely visible specks, and the roofs of other skyscrapers sit far beneath the slide. Dark storm cloud presses down on the skyline and fine rain streaks through the air. Electric-blue LED strips trace both wet rims of the chute and around its flared mouth, spilling cyan light onto the sheet of water running down the floor and onto the rider's skin. Heavy steel trusses and anchor bolts hold the structure to the roof deck. The chute pitches away almost vertically from the lip, and the full drop is visible in the first frame.

The entire video is one continuous first-person shot from an action camera mounted at the rider's chest and looking down the body in the direction of travel. The rider's thighs, lower legs and bare feet stay visible in the lower portion of the frame from the first frame to the last. The camera banks with the body through every curve, pitches down with the slide through every drop, and carries constant subtle high-frequency vibration from the speed. It never detaches from the rider, never cuts, never switches to a third-person or drone angle, never orbits, never looks backward, and never jumps to a different part of the slide. There is no approach, no walking, no stairs and no establishing shot - the video begins with the rider already in position at the edge.

Begin with the rider completely stationary at the very top of the slide, legs and bare feet visible at the bottom of the first-person frame. The POV deliberately tilts downward over the edge for a brief moment, revealing the city streets impossibly far below and making the enormous vertical height immediately understandable. Hold this vertigo-inducing view long enough for the viewer to register the danger. The rider's legs subtly tense in hesitation. Then the rider suddenly commits, making one small forward hop and push from the deck into the flowing transparent chute. The POV jolts and dips with the body movement, the legs extend forward, and gravity immediately catches the rider, transforming the brief stillness into violent downward acceleration.

TIMELINE

0:00-0:01.4
Completely still. The POV tilts down over the edge of the deck. The deck's edge crosses the middle of frame and past it there is nothing - four hundred metres of open air, then the city. Nothing moves but the water pouring off the slide's lip and disappearing.

0:01.4-0:02.0
The bare feet shift once on the wet glass and settle closer together. The calves tense. The body leans a few centimetres further forward, the view over the edge opens slightly wider, and it stops there.

0:02.0-0:02.5
The rider commits - one small forward hop off the deck into the mouth of the chute. The POV dips and jolts hard, the legs extend forward, there is a fraction of a second of weightlessness, and the blue-lit transparent chute floods the frame.

0:02.5-0:05.0
Near-vertical descent down the glass face of the tower. The chute pitches past 75 degrees, the horizon snaps to the top edge of frame, and the city fills the lower two-thirds. Water rushes up around the calves in white ribbons, spray flares off both walls into the lower corners, and the rider's feet lift briefly before slamming back into the water. Lit office windows blur past on the left through the transparent wall. The blue LED rims converge to a single vanishing point ahead.

0:05.0-0:08.0
The chute flattens for half a second, then rolls into a long banked curve that wraps around the outside of the tower. The whole frame rolls with it; the legs slide toward the outer wall and water piles up on the high side. The curve opens onto an exposed straight running out into open air between two towers, with nothing below the transparent floor but three hundred metres of rain and rooftops. A second drop takes the rider down into the tower cluster.

0:08.0-0:11.0
Lightning fires down through the cloud onto a neighbouring tower, whiting out the sky and lighting the wet transparent chute from the outside for a fraction of a second. In the flash the whole city is briefly legible - water tanks, parapets, aerials, a helicopter crossing far below the slide. The chute banks hard right around a building corner, spray sheeting off the outer wall, then drops again into the gap between two glass façades with the rider's own reflection racing alongside.

0:11.0-0:13.7
The route straightens into its steepest run yet, dropping into the street canyon with lit walls rising on both sides. Streetlights and window grids stream past as pure horizontal smear. Far ahead and closing fast, a small glowing turquoise rectangle resolves into an infinity pool on a lower rooftop terrace. Spray builds until it half-blinds the lens.

0:13.7-0:15.0
The POV hits the water. Huge realistic splash, whiteout of foam and bubbles, deceleration, then the surface settles and the rider's bare feet float in glowing turquoise water with the dark towers and storm sky standing all around. The take has still not cut.

CAMERA BEHAVIOR
For the first two beats the camera behaves like the rider's head rather than a mounted rig: still, angled down over the edge, drifting only as much as a person deciding whether to go, and never aligned straight down the chute. At the hop it dips and jolts sharply with the body. From that point on the camera is body-mounted and moves only as the rider moves. On each drop it pitches down with the chute so the horizon rides the top edge of frame; on each banked turn the whole frame rolls with the body. Vibration and motion blur increase with speed and drop back on the flatter sections. Spray sweeps across the lens repeatedly and beads on it briefly before clearing. Focus stays wide and deep with slight natural barrel distortion at the frame edges.

CONTINUITY & MOTION RULES
- One continuous first-person take from start to finish, no cuts.
- The rider is completely stationary until the hop; no forward travel of any kind before it.
- The hop is one small human push-off, never a leap, dive, run-up or jump into open air.
- Vibration and motion blur are near zero before the hop and ramp up only after it.
- The rider's two legs and two bare feet stay visible and anatomically correct throughout.
- The slide is a single continuous connected structure; every section physically joins the one before it.
- Water flows down the chute and sprays off the walls without interruption.
- The city stays visible beside, beyond and beneath the transparent slide for the whole ride.
- Acceleration is gravity-driven: slow over the lip, violent through each drop, sustained fast between them.
- Photorealistic lighting, reflections, water physics and skin; real-world scale and atmospheric depth.
- Lightning strikes at most twice; the sky is not continuously lit.
- No walking toward the slide, no stairs, no standing around, no establishing shot, no third-person or drone angle.
- The video does not open mid-slide or already in motion, and the opening framing never lets the slide structure hide the drop below.
- No cuts, no camera detaching from the rider, no teleporting between slide sections.
- No floating slide pieces without visible support; no sudden environment change.
- No duplicated, deformed, extra or missing limbs; no hands entering frame; the rider never turns around.
- No dry slide, no static water, no slow casual descent, no low theme-park scale.
- No CGI, video-game or cartoon appearance; no text or watermark.
```

## What the four have in common

Read them side by side and the constants are obvious:

- **Frame one is identical in structure every time** — feet at the bottom, the platform edge across the middle, the chute mouth small and just ahead, an enormous drop beyond. Only the world changes.
- **Nothing moves until the hop.** Every build spends its first beat completely still with the POV tilted down over the edge, and every one names the exact moment the rider commits.
- **The opening sequence paragraph is word-for-word the same skeleton** in all four — only the altitude cue and the surface being hopped off change. That repetition is deliberate: it is the part the video models most reliably drop.
- **Every altitude cue is concrete** — a helicopter below the slide, an expedition tent as a dot, a river as a thread, traffic as specks, birds turning below the rider's own feet. Never "very high".
- **The slide is small in the opening frame and large after the hop.** None of them opens with chute walls filling the shot.
- **The second spectacle is always local to the environment** — lightning, a waterfall pass, a shaft of sunset light through a slot, an open crevasse under a transparent floor. Never interchangeable.
- **The environment is visible through the slide**, not merely behind it.
- **Every ride ends in water**, with the splashdown as its own timestamped beat and no cut.
- **Duration buys ride, not setup.** The 20-second build has four mid-ride features; the 10-second build has one. Both carry all eight beats, and both give the vertigo reveal at least a full second.
