# POV Water Slide — Ride Logic

# Ride Logic

The eight beats, the three route shapes, the slide itself, and how each environment rewrites all of it.

---

## The eight beats

Every ride, every duration, every environment. In this order, all present.

The first three carry the whole emotional load. They are the beats the primary viral reference has and this skill's earlier output did not.

### 1 — VERTIGO REVEAL · frame one, and nothing is moving yet

The rider is **already at the edge** and **completely stationary**. Legs and bare feet in the lower portion of frame, standing or seated on wet deck, glass floor, bare rock or steel grating. **The edge of that platform is visible**, and past it there is nothing.

The POV then deliberately **tilts down over the edge** — a small forward lean of the head, not a rig move — and the world far below comes into frame.

Frame division:

```
world far below, past the edge      ~35–45%   the largest single share
rider's legs, feet and platform     ~35–45%
the slide entrance itself           ~10–15%   small, on the seam between them
sky                                 a thin band, or none
```

Write this beat with **specific altitude cues** — things whose real size the viewer knows, rendered tiny. Cars as crawling specks. Streets as thin threads. A helicopter passing *below* the platform. Treetops as texture. A waterfall reduced to a white line. Cloud sitting underneath the deck. Vague words like "very high" do nothing; a bus the size of a grain of rice does everything.

**Hold it long enough to land.** The viewer has to get all the way to *"oh — that is how far down this goes."* The goal is not to show a beautiful view; it is to make the viewer feel how far they could fall.

**Duration: ~1.0 s at 10 s · ~1.4 s at 15 s · ~1.6 s at 20 s. Never cut it.**

### 2 — HESITATION

Tiny human body language, still without travel: the bare feet shift once and settle closer together, the calves and thighs tense, one knee repositions, the body leans a few centimetres further forward and stops. The camera micro-drifts the way a real head does when someone is deciding.

**Subtle. Never theatrical.** No shaking, no recoiling, no stepping back. This beat reads as *hesitation*, not as *panic*.

**Duration: ~0.4 s at 10 s · ~0.6 s at 15 s · ~0.7 s at 20 s.**

### 3 — COMMIT + HOP

The rider decides and moves. **One small, physically believable forward hop, push-off or committed scoot** from the platform into the flowing chute. The movement of a person who has just thought *"I've decided — go."*

The camera must react to it physically: the body shifts suddenly forward, the legs extend, the POV **dips and jolts** vertically, there is a fractional moment of weightlessness, and the slide rapidly fills more of the frame.

**Never a leap into open air, a dive, a run-up, or a theatrical jump.** It is a small human movement, and its whole job is to be the visible hinge between stillness and falling.

**Duration: ~0.4 s at 10 s · ~0.5 s at 15 s · ~0.5 s at 20 s.**

### 4 — FIRST DROP · gravity catches

The defining beat. **Near-vertical, exposed, terrifying.**

It opens on the hop landing: gravity takes the rider, and the difference between this beat and the three before it must be unmistakable on screen. Stillness becomes violence in well under half a second.

The chute pitches past 65–80 degrees. The horizon snaps to the top edge of frame. Everything below fills the rest. Water rushes up around the legs, spray flares off both walls, motion blur ramps hard, the action-camera vibration starts. The rider's feet lift slightly and slam back down into the water.

**This beat is non-negotiable regardless of route choice.** Even the "Curved / Twisting" route opens with it.

**Duration: 15–20 % of runtime.**

### 5 — MID-RIDE

Where the route shape and the environment do their work. Pick features from the environment pack below and chain them so each flows physically into the next:

banked curve · long exposed straight · secondary drop · S-curve · partially enclosed transparent section · a pass close to something huge · a height re-reveal.

**Every feature must connect to the one before it.** The rider is on one continuous piece of geometry the whole time.

**Duration: 20–25 % of runtime.**

### 6 — SECOND SPECTACLE

One big, singular, environment-specific event. The moment the viewer would replay. Never generic and never a second copy of the first drop.

**Duration: 15–20 % of runtime.**

### 7 — FINAL DESCENT

One last acceleration, steeper and faster than anything since the first drop, aimed at the destination. The destination is **visible ahead and closing** — a small bright rectangle of pool, a lake, a river, an ocean surface. Spray builds until it half-blinds the lens.

**Duration: 12–18 % of runtime.**

### 8 — SPLASHDOWN

The continuous POV hits the water. Huge realistic splash, whiteout of foam and bubbles, then a fast settle. Optional final second: feet floating at the surface with the environment standing around, camera still rolling. **No cut, ever, including here.**

**Duration: 8–12 % of runtime.**

## The three route shapes

The user picks one. All three keep beat 3 identical.

### A — EXTREME STRAIGHT DROP

Maximum vertigo, minimum lateral movement. A near-vertical exposed plunge, a short flattening, then a second and third drop, each steeper than the last. Barely any turning. The whole video is falling.

Best in: canyon, glacier, volcanic, desert · any environment with one enormous single vertical face.
Mid-ride features: stepped drops · a brief flat "breath" section that makes the next drop worse · an overhang where the chute leaves the rock face entirely and hangs in open air.

### B — CURVED / TWISTING

Steep first drop, then aggressive sweeping banked turns, S-curves, spirals and long carving arcs. Centrifugal force is the sensation. Height gets re-revealed each time a turn opens the view.

Best in: city, tropical, mountains · anywhere with structures or terrain to wrap around.
Mid-ride features: 180° banked hairpins · a spiral around a tower or rock spire · high-wall carving turns where the rider rides up the outer wall · linked S-curves.

### C — HYBRID *(default — recommend this)*

Both, alternating. Near-vertical drop → banked curve → exposed straight → S-curve → drop → partially enclosed transparent section → final plunge. The most visual variety per second, and the closest match to the primary reference.

**Use Hybrid whenever the user does not choose, says "surprise me", or picks something ambiguous.**

## The slide

**Default construction:** a large transparent or translucent chute, wide enough for exactly one rider, with wet reflective surfaces, high curved side walls converging to a V ahead of the feet, a continuous sheet of water running down the floor, and **believable structural support** — visible steel trusses, anchor points bolted into rock or concrete, cable stays, pylons standing on rooftops. The slide is impossible in scale, never in engineering.

**Seen from inside**, the chute reads as: two bright converging edge lines, a flowing water floor, and the world beyond, distorted slightly by the wet transparent walls.

### LED lighting

| Choice | How to write it |
|---|---|
| **No LEDs** | Clear or faintly tinted acrylic. The environment lights the slide. Best in daylight, golden hour and snow. |
| **Blue** | Electric-blue strips along both upper rims and around the mouth. Cyan spill onto the water, the wet walls and the skin. *The original.* |
| **Red** | Deep red strips; the whole chute interior glows warm. Spray and legs read warm. Strongest at blue hour and night. |
| **Purple** | Violet rims with magenta spill. Reads futuristic against a cool night city. |
| **Green** | Emerald strips. Best over jungle, forest, aurora skies. |
| **White** | Cold bright strips, almost clinical. Best on glacier, snow and fog. |
| **Custom** | Whatever they name. Same rules. |

**LED rules, always:**

- Strips **follow the geometry** — they trace the rims, the curve of the mouth, the banking of each turn. They never float free.
- The colour **spills**. Onto the flowing water, the wet walls, the spray, the rider's legs and feet. An LED colour that only lights the trim is a failure.
- In darkness the strips are the **only continuous line** in the frame, converging to a vanishing point. That convergence is what sells speed.

## Environment packs

The user's environment choice rewrites the **whole ride**, not the backdrop. Never bolt a new sky onto a city route.

**The "altitude cues" line in each pack is the raw material for beat 1.** Those are the specific small things the downward tilt has to reveal — the objects whose real-world size the viewer already knows, rendered tiny. Pick two or three and name them concretely in the vertigo reveal. The environment beneath the rider is not scenery in this format; it is the fear mechanism.

### CITY

**Launch anchor** the roof or a glass observation deck of an enormous supertall tower.
**Altitude cues** avenues as thin chains of light · cars as crawling specks · other skyscrapers *below* the slide · a helicopter passing underneath · cloud shredding around the tower.
**Route** down the face of the tower · between two towers · a banked curve wrapping around a building · a rooftop-level run over water tanks and parapets · a gap-crossing between rooftops · a final descent into a street canyon with walls on both sides.
**Second spectacle** lightning striking a neighbouring tower · bursting through a low cloud layer between buildings · a curve so tight the chute grazes a glass façade with the rider's reflection racing alongside.
**Destination** rooftop infinity pool · a lit plaza pool at street level · a pool on a lower setback terrace.
**Palette** orange sodium streets, cold blue-white office windows, wet black glass, navy or storm-grey sky.

### MOUNTAINS

**Launch anchor** a bare rock ledge or a small anchored platform on a summit or cliff shoulder.
**Altitude cues** the valley floor as a dark carpet of forest · a river as a thin silver thread · a waterfall reduced to a white line · cloud sitting below the platform · another peak across the void.
**Route** straight down a cliff face with rock streaming past at arm's length · out over open air on a cable-stayed span · past a waterfall · curving around a rock buttress · through a mist band · along a ridge line between two peaks.
**Second spectacle** a full waterfall pass with spray across the lens · a cloud-band blackout and the burst back into light · the chute leaving the rock and hanging over a kilometre of nothing.
**Destination** an alpine lake · a plunge pool at the base of a waterfall · a glacial tarn.
**Palette** granite grey and gold, dark conifer green, white water, cold blue shadow.

### CANYON

**Launch anchor** the very edge of a canyon rim, red rock, a thin steel frame anchored into the stone.
**Altitude cues** the river a thread at the bottom · layered strata shrinking with depth · birds circling below · the far rim a kilometre away.
**Route** down the vertical rim wall · into the narrowing slot · through a slot-canyon section where walls close to within a metre on both sides · out into a sudden open bowl · a banked curve against the canyon wall.
**Second spectacle** the slot canyon opening into an enormous void with no wall on either side · a shaft of light coming down through the slot · crossing the full canyon width on a single unsupported-looking span.
**Destination** the canyon river · a green plunge pool at the base · a natural rock basin.
**Palette** burnt orange, rust, deep purple shadow, bright turquoise water.

### TROPICAL / JUNGLE

**Launch anchor** the top of a limestone karst tower or a platform above the canopy.
**Altitude cues** the canopy as a green texture · birds flying below · a river winding through the trees · sea stacks in a distant bay.
**Route** a plunge past the karst wall · a run just above the canopy with leaves brushing the chute · through a curtain waterfall · a spiral around a limestone spire · out over a turquoise lagoon.
**Second spectacle** bursting straight through a waterfall curtain · skimming the canopy so close that birds scatter · the lagoon opening up all at once.
**Destination** a turquoise lagoon · a jungle river · a cenote · the ocean.
**Palette** saturated green, warm limestone, turquoise water, humid haze.

### GLACIER / SNOW

**Launch anchor** the lip of an ice cliff or a serac field, frame anchored in blue ice.
**Altitude cues** crevasse fields shrinking below · a moraine as a grey ribbon · a distant expedition tent as a dot.
**Route** down a blue-ice wall · through an ice arch · alongside a crevasse deep enough to lose the bottom in · over a snowfield · past seracs the size of buildings.
**Second spectacle** the chute passing directly over an open crevasse with the blue depth visible through the transparent floor · an ice collapse in the distance · sun flaring through translucent ice.
**Destination** a meltwater lake · a glacial lagoon with floating ice · a hole cut into the ice.
**Palette** white, cyan ice-blue, hard sun, long blue shadows.

### DESERT

**Launch anchor** the top of an enormous mesa or a lone rock tower.
**Altitude cues** dunes as ripples · a road as a thin scar · a vehicle as a speck with a dust plume.
**Route** down the sheer mesa face · out over open dunes · a banked curve around a rock tower · a long low run skimming the sand · a final drop into a hidden oasis.
**Second spectacle** a distant dust wall or haboob rolling in · the oasis appearing suddenly out of the dunes · the chute crossing a slot between two mesas.
**Destination** an oasis pool · a desert reservoir · a hidden canyon spring.
**Palette** ochre, rose, hard shadow, dusty haze at distance.

### VOLCANIC

**Launch anchor** the crater rim, black basalt, steel anchored into cooled lava.
**Altitude cues** the crater floor far below · steam vents as small plumes · lava as thin orange veins.
**Route** down the outer cone · past active vents · over a lava field with orange cracks visible through the transparent floor · through a steam curtain · around a spatter cone.
**Second spectacle** the chute crossing directly above a lava channel · a vent erupting alongside the route · steam blackout and the burst back into light.
**Destination** a black-sand coastal pool · a geothermal lagoon · the ocean at the lava delta.
**Palette** black rock, ember orange, sulphur yellow, grey steam, ash sky.

### CUSTOM

Build the same eight-beat structure from whatever they describe. Ask only for the environment itself, then decide the launch anchor, altitude cues, route features, spectacle and destination yourself.

## Time of day

| | Light | Best with |
|---|---|---|
| **Day** | hard sun, sharp shadows, deep blue sky, brilliant white water | mountains, canyon, glacier, tropical |
| **Golden Hour** | low warm sun, long shadows, rim-lit spray, glowing rock | mountains, desert, canyon |
| **Sunset** | orange-to-violet gradient, silhouetted terrain, warm reflections in the chute | city, ocean, desert |
| **Blue Hour** | deep blue sky, city lights just coming up, cool ambient | city, glacier — pairs best with red or white LEDs |
| **Night** | environment lit only by its own lights, LEDs dominant, wet reflections everywhere | city — the original |

---

## Weather

| | What it adds |
|---|---|
| **Clear** | maximum visible distance — the strongest choice for pure height |
| **Cloudy** | a ceiling that makes the height feel enclosed and heavy |
| **Fog / Mist** | a blackout band mid-ride and a violent reveal coming out of it |
| **Rain** | streaks through the air, water beading and running on the transparent walls, everything reflective |
| **Thunderstorm + Lightning** | the strongest second-spectacle generator; one or two strikes, never continuous |
| **Snow** | flakes streaming past the lens horizontally at speed |

**Lightning rule:** one strike in the mid-ride, at most one more later. It briefly lights the whole environment and the wet slide from outside. Continuous lightning reads as CGI.

## End destinations

| | Write it as |
|---|---|
| **Swimming Pool** | a lit rectangle at ground level, small and bright, growing fast |
| **Rooftop Pool** | an infinity pool on a lower tower with the city still around and below it |
| **Mountain Lake** | dark still water ringed by rock and conifers |
| **Ocean** | open water, surf line, the horizon rising as the chute drops |
| **River** | moving water with visible current, banks streaming past |
| **Waterfall Basin** | churning white plunge pool with the fall roaring alongside |

The destination must be **visible ahead during the final descent** and must belong to the same world as the launch. A rooftop pool does not appear in a canyon.

## Scaling to duration

| Beat | 10 s | 15 s | 20 s |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vertigo reveal | 1.0 s | 1.4 s | 1.6 s |
| Hesitation | 0.4 s | 0.6 s | 0.7 s |
| Commit + hop | 0.4 s | 0.5 s | 0.5 s |
| First drop | 1.6 s | 2.5 s | 3.2 s |
| Mid-ride | 2.0 s | 3.0 s | 4.2 s |
| Second spectacle | 1.8 s | 3.0 s | 4.0 s |
| Final descent | 1.8 s | 2.7 s | 4.2 s |
| Splashdown | 1.0 s | 1.3 s | 1.6 s |

**The opening three beats are protected.** They are roughly 18 % of a 10-second video and 14 % of a 20-second one — the reveal stays a real beat at every length, and a short duration compresses the ride, not the fear.

**Extra seconds never buy a longer hold at the top.** They go into mid-ride features and the second spectacle. Extra length means **more slide**.

For a custom duration keep the same proportions, and never let the vertigo reveal fall below one second.
