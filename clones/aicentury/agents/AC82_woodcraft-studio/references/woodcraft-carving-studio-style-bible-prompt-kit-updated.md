The Locked Woodcraft Style (never changes)Reuse this Style Line verbatim in every prompt:a cinematic ASMR woodcarving montage in one continuous real workshop session — same hands, same worn wooden workbench, same warm directional lighting throughout; solid natural hardwood keeping its NATURAL colour, grain and texture the whole way (never painted); clean real adult hands, natural grip and tool contact; hand tools only — pencil, ruler, carving knife, mallet and chisels, gouge, fine scoring blade, a small hand drill, sandpaper, a soft brush, and wood oil with a cloth/applicator pad; ultra-photorealistic, not CGI, not a 3D render, not stylized.
Pacing: a slow, satisfying time-lapse in continuous real workshop time — fast enough to show progress, slow enough that every cut, joint and wipe is clearly visible and believable.3. Universal Negatives (add to every prompt)no captions, no subtitles, no on-screen text, no watermark, no plastic or 3D-render look, no CGI, no time skips, no self-carving/self-assembly/morphing, no parts flying or merging on their own, no floating/disconnected motion, no tool affecting an area it isn't touching, no paint of any kind (no colored/opaque/automotive/chrome/glossy painted coating, no artificial colour coat), no real full-sized object, no street/city/showroom/real-world scene, no cluttered background, no aspect ratio.4. The Finish = WOOD OIL, applied by hand (never paint)The previous paint/airbrush finish is removed. The wooden craft never receives colored paint, opaque paint, automotive paint, chrome paint, an artificial colour coating, or a glossy painted finish. The final object must remain visibly real wood — natural grain, natural colour variation, carved detail and material texture all clearly visible.Instead, the final stage is a natural wood-oil finish applied by hand, using a clean cloth, a soft applicator pad, a small brush, or a controlled wood-oil spray. It has visible cause and effect:1. The untreated wooden surface initially looks dry and matte.2. The hand applies a small amount of wood oil.3. The cloth / brush / applicator / spray moves directly across the surface.4. Only the areas touched by the oil become slightly darker and richer.5. The oil is spread evenly across the object.6. The hand wipes away any excess oil.7. The wood grain becomes more defined with a subtle natural sheen.The finish must not appear instantly across the whole object, and there is no automatic dry!finished transformation. The result should look like real wood carefully sanded and finished with oil — a subtle sheen, not a thick glossy coating.
5. Separate Components + Visible AssemblyThe video must not show one block instantly becoming the whole object. Divide the subject into logical components — the GPT decides which parts should logically be carved separately based on the subject:¥Vehicle: body from the main block; wheels, axles, mirrors, spoiler carved from their own small pieces.¥Animal: main body from one block; legs, ears, horns, wings, tail from smaller pieces.¥Other subjects: handles, doors, decorative panels, structural details, etc.Each separate component is physically created from its own piece of wood — never appearing already completed. After it's made, it stays visible on the bench until it's attached.Show how the parts connect (close-ups, easy for the viewer to follow) — never a jump from loose parts to a finished object:¥holes being drilled,¥pegs/dowels being prepared,¥glue being applied,¥parts being aligned,¥joints/slots sliding together,¥hands pressing components into position,¥the assembled connection being checked.Realistic connection methods: wooden pegs, dowels, carved slots, fitted joints, small axle pins, wood glue, or carefully aligned connection points. The hand must visibly place, align, press, insert, or glue every component. Every connection must look physically possible and structurally believable.6. Strict Chronological Order, No Morphing, No Premature RevealStrict order — the object only ever moves FORWARD. Never show a finished component before its raw material is prepared, sanding before the main shape is carved, attached parts that later appear separate again, completed details that vanish next shot, oil applied before full assembly, or a nearly-complete sculpture halfway through. It must never become 
less complete in a later shot. Every shot continues the exact physical progress of the previous shot.No morphing / no self-assembly. Do not allow: parts flying toward the object, separate pieces merging automatically, surfaces reshaping without tool contact, components snapping into place by themselves, the sculpture becoming more complete between unrelated frames, hidden off-screen assembly, or any magical AI-style transformation. Every important construction change is performed visibly by the hands.No premature reveal. Keep it visibly unfinished through the first ~two-thirds — raw material, rough shapes, separate unfinished components, exposed connection points. The complete sculpture appears only near the end, so the final reveal feels earned.7. Continuity & PersistenceKeep consistent the whole video: the same adult hands, the same wood type, the same grain and colour, the same object scale, the same workbench, the same workshop, the same tools, the same lighting, and the same physical progress.¥Separate components remain visible on the table after they're created until they're attached.¥Wood shavings and dust accumulate naturally until the cleanup stage.¥Nothing disappears, resets, or changes position without a believable physical reason.8. Camera & SoundDifferent camera angles keep it engaging, but it must still feel like one continuous session: overhead workbench, side close-up, three-quarter, macro tool-contact (carving / drilling / oil-wipe), close sanding, close glue/joint, and a final product angle. The object, hands, bench, lighting and progress stay consistent; smooth deliberate motion, no floating/disconnected movement, no jump cuts.Sound (diegetic ASMR only): chisel taps, wood scraping/splintering, hand-drill, sandpaper friction, soft brush sweeping, the soft wipe of the oil cloth. No music, no voice-over.
9. The 10-Stage Sequence (build in this order)1. Raw materials. The main raw wood block and the smaller wood pieces that will become separate components — all rough, unfinished, unshaped.2. Planning & measuring. Hands sketch outlines, measure, and mark carving guides directly on the wood.3. Cutting the main form. The main block is cut / roughly carved into the basic foundation — still very rough and incomplete.4. Creating separate components. Close-ups of the smaller parts being made, each from its own piece of wood (wheels, legs, mirrors, tail, etc.). Never already-completed.5. Refining the main body. Continue carving/shaping the main form; proportions clearer but still visibly unfinished.6. Refining the separate parts. The smaller pieces are carved, sanded, drilled or shaped, ready to connect.7. Joining the components. Show how parts attach — pegs, dowels, slots, fitted joints, axle pins, or glue — the hand visibly places, aligns, presses, inserts or glues each one.8. Final sanding & detail. After assembly, final sanding, detail carving, and surface cleanup.9. Cleaning. Wood dust and shavings brushed off the craft and the bench.10. Wood-oil finish. Hands apply wood oil gradually (Section 4), enhancing the natural grain and completing the handcrafted look — end on the finished natural wooden object.Merge sensibly for very simple objects, but keep the order and never skip raw materials, separate components, visible joining, cleanup, or the oil finish.9b.
