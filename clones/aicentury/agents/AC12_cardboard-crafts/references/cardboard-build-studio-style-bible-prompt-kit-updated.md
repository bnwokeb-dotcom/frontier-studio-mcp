Core Rule 1 — Length, Pacing & SuspenseEvery build is 15 seconds and paced for anticipation.1. Stay obviously unfinished for the first ~13 seconds — show loose cut pieces, a partial frame, incomplete/missing panels, exposed internals and 
visible gaps. The recognizable design stays hidden.2. The defining outer panels (the ones that reveal *what it is*) go on only in the last ~2 seconds; the finished model appears only in the last ~1 second. Never let the silhouette read as near-complete earlier, and never linger on the finished model for more than ~1s.The pace is slow, chronological and satisfying. Suspense is the point: withhold the design until the final reveal so the ending lands.Why: early prototypes revealed the finished car by the halfway mark and then just rotated it — killing the anticipation and wasting half the runtime.5. Core Rule 2 — Structure First, Skin Last (the Build Arc)Split every object into two things:¥STRUCTURE — the frame, chassis, internals, supports, ribs. Built throughout the middle and kept deliberately unfinished-looking (exposed corrugated edges, gaps, missing skin).¥SKIN — the defining outer panels that make it recognizable (nose, hood, doors, roofline, signature curves). Added only at the very end.Run every build through 6Ð8 chronological stages (never fewer than 6). Default flow for a vehicle:1. Loose cut pieces + tools laid out.2. Floor pan + chassis rails/bulkheads — a bare frame.3. Wheels on exposed axles + supports — skeletal, open.4. Interior tub / firewall — internals exposed.5. Roll-cage / pillars / roof ribs — see-through, no panels.6. First partial panels tacked on with obvious gaps.7. Final ~2s: hands add the remaining outer skin one piece at a time (nose, hood, doors, defining curves).8. Short rotating reveal — the full design seen for the first time.Adapt the parts to the subject (a guitar has a body core + neck + soundboard; a plane has a keel + wing ribs + nacelles + skin), but keep the same logic: build the skeleton, hide the silhouette, skin it last.
6. Core Rule 3 — Hands Drive Every Change (physical causality)The construction must be physically believable from start to finish, like a real stop-motion handcraft build.¥Every new piece appears only because a visible hand places, folds, glues, aligns, presses or attaches it.¥Keep a hand in frame at every construction change.¥Nothing flies, snaps into place, merges, morphs or self-assembles. The only motion between states is caused by the hand.¥The viewer must always see how each part is added — clear cause and effect.Why: a prototype "skinned" the model with a hands-free AI morph — panels folded and closed over the frame by themselves. That must never happen. Write every beat as a hand action ("hands place and press the panelÉ"), never "the panel comes together."7. Camera VarietyGive each stage its own camera angle so a long build stays engaging, while the model stays centered and continuous.¥Rotate through: overhead top-down, low angle, side profile, front three-quarter, high three-quarter, tight macro close-up, slow rotating orbit.¥While unfinished, favour angles that show the exposed frame and internals (overhead into an open cabin, low angle through a bare chassis, macro on a joint) — not flattering hero angles that would give the silhouette away early.¥Keep the acting hand visible. Smooth, deliberate moves. Never jump cuts.¥Diegetic sound only: knife slicing cardboard, hot-glue-gun hiss, soft cardboard taps and rustle, quiet room tone. No music, no voice-over.8.
