## Primary Reference A — Bull/cow

**Duration** 10.03 s · 30 fps · 9:16 · single continuous take, no cuts.

**Environment.** Dry tropical scrubland in hard midday sun. A pale dirt track running left-to-right through a shallow gully, a steep eroded earth bank on the far side, leafless grey trees, deep blue sky. Sharp black shadows directly under the animal.

**Camera.** Handheld smartphone in profile to the action, **tracking laterally** at roughly the animal's speed — the operator is moving alongside, not standing still. Framing is loose and imperfect: the bull drifts within the frame, the horizon tips, focus breathes. At launch the camera tilts up and widens off the subjects to hold both in frame; through the airborne phase it holds a wide, sky-heavy composition with the animal low and the rider high; on the descent it tilts and pans back down; after the landing it swings around behind the pair and settles as they run away down the track. Nothing about the operator's framing anticipates the choreography — the reactions arrive slightly late, which is exactly what sells it.

**Approach — 0:00 to 0:03.2.** The rider is **already standing upright on the bull's back** in a surf stance, knees soft, both arms extended for balance, barefoot. The bull — a brown zebu-type with a shoulder hump and short forward horns — gallops right along the dirt track. Frame one already contains rider, animal, contact, forward motion and environment. There is no establishing shot.

**Ramp — 0:03.2 to 0:03.9.** The launch structure is **improvised and man-made**: a long sheet of rust-red corrugated metal laid at roughly 25–30° over the collapsed remains of a shack — an oil drum, a metal crate, scattered timber and rubble underneath. It enters frame from the right at 0:03.3, seconds before it is used, so the viewer sees where the upward momentum comes from. At 0:03.5 the bull's front hooves strike the lower lip; it runs up the sheet, dust peeling off behind. The rider drops into a deep crouch, hands down at the withers, and compresses.

**Launch and separation — 0:03.9 to 0:04.2.** As the bull reaches the crest the rider **extends hard upward and throws his head and shoulders backward**, timing his own jump to the animal's takeoff. This is the single most important mechanical detail in the whole reference: the rider is not passively thrown off — he actively launches, which is what buys him the height and the rotation. The bull leaves the ramp nose-up, front legs folding. By 0:04.0 the two are separate; by 0:04.1 the rider is inverted, head down, above and slightly behind the bull.

**Aerial phase — 0:04.2 to 0:07.2.** Two rotations, different speeds, one shared arc.
The **bull** performs a slow, complete backward somersault: nose up, over onto its back with all four legs pointing up-frame, continuing round toward feet-down. It stays one continuous body, never morphing.
The **rider** performs a faster backward rotation above it — layout into a tight tuck (knees to chest, head tucked) and around, roughly one and a half to two rotations across the phase.
Throughout, the rider stays **directly above the bull with one to two body-lengths of clear air between them**. They are never in the same place, and never far apart. The spatial relationship is legible in every single frame.

**Descent and remount — 0:07.2 to 0:08.9.** The bull comes out of its rotation feet-down and descends toward the track. The rider opens out of the tuck, brings his feet under him and drops **straight down onto the bull's back**. Contact at ~0:08.9 is **feet-first onto the back just behind the shoulders**, while the bull's own hooves are touching down — they land as a unit. The rider immediately folds into a low crouch, hands down on the withers, absorbing.

**Landing and continuation — 0:08.9 to 0:10.0.** The bull gallops on down the dirt track, kicking a dust trail, rider crouched and stable, then rising slightly as balance returns. The camera pans right and settles behind them. It ends shortly after the successful landing, with no second action.

## Primary Reference B — Elephant

**Duration** 10.07 s · 30 fps · 9:16 · single continuous take, no cuts.

**Environment.** African savanna under a flat overcast sky. Pale gold dry grass, scattered flat-topped acacias to the horizon, loose dust that lifts at every footfall.

**Camera.** Handheld smartphone shot **from inside a safari vehicle**, looking out over the bodywork. The vehicle's canopy edge cuts a hard dark diagonal across the top of the frame; the roll bar and windscreen frame cross the bottom; the heads of two other passengers — one in a green cap with an adidas logo — rise into the lower frame and partially occlude the action. The camera is broadly **behind** the animal, so the stunt runs away from the viewer. It pans and tilts to keep the elephant centered, tilts up hard at the launch, holds wide through the airborne phase, then tilts down for the landing and takes a small shake at the impact. The foreground occlusion is a large part of why the shot reads as genuine bystander footage.

**Approach — 0:00 to 0:03.2.** The rider is **already crouched low on the elephant's back**, just behind the head, hands down. The elephant — a full-grown African elephant with visible tusks and large ears — runs directly away from camera across the savanna, dust bursting from each foot. **(This rear-facing camera position is documented here because it is what the clip shows — it is not to be reproduced. Builds use the lateral side profile of Reference A. See the camera note below.)** A conical earth mound becomes visible ahead and right at ~0:02.0. Again: frame one is already the stunt.

**Ramp — 0:03.2 to 0:04.1.** The launch structure is **natural and environmental**: a large conical dirt mound of loose dry earth, roughly as tall as the elephant, steep-sided. The elephant runs straight at the near face and climbs it, rear legs driving, dust sheeting off the flanks. The climb is slow and heavy — an elephant's weight is visible in it.

**Launch and separation — 0:04.1 to 0:04.4.** At the crest the elephant launches nose-up off the top. The rider stands and **extends upward off its back** at the same instant, separating cleanly. Same mechanic as Reference A.

**Aerial phase — 0:04.4 to 0:07.2.** The elephant performs a slow full backward somersault — pitching up, rolling over onto its back with legs and trunk up, coming round toward feet-down. Its rotation is **noticeably slower and heavier than the bull's**, which is correct: the mass is doing the work. The rider rotates backward faster above it, layout into a tuck. Again the rider holds station directly above the elephant with clear air between them for the whole phase.

**Descent and remount — 0:07.2 to 0:07.9.** The elephant comes round feet-down and lands **rear feet first**, front legs extended, trunk swinging. In the same beat the rider opens out of the tuck, body vertical and feet down, arms reaching forward, and drops onto the elephant's back behind the head. Contact is feet-first; he immediately drops into a low crouch with both hands down and absorbs.

**Landing and continuation — 0:07.9 to 0:10.0.** The elephant's touchdown throws a **massive dust burst** that grows until it nearly fills the frame. The elephant runs forward through it with the rider crouched and mounted. The camera holds, the passenger's cap bobs in the foreground, and the clip ends inside the dust.

## Secondary Reference — Crocodile

**Duration** 10.07 s · 30 fps · 9:16 · single continuous take.

**Environment.** A brown swamp river, dense palm and cypress on the far bank, floating hyacinth, humid grey light.

**Camera.** Handheld smartphone from a **moving boat** — lateral tracking in profile, closely matching Reference A's approach. Another passenger's phone appears in the bottom corner of frame at ~0:07.0, and the boat's rail crosses the bottom of the last shots. Same eyewitness grammar.

**What it shares with the primaries — 0:00 to 0:06.0.** The rider is already standing on the crocodile's back in a surf stance, arms out, as it swims. The animal reaches a **steep mud bank** — an environmental, improvised ramp — and drives up it. Rider and animal launch together, the rider separates upward, both rotate through the air with the rider held high above the animal. Through this stretch the grammar is identical to A and B.

**Where it diverges — 0:06.0 onward.** The crocodile crashes into the water instead of onto land. It then **breaches vertically out of the river**, jaws wide, rising almost its full length above the surface with the rider on its head. It falls back in a heavy splash and swims away. **The rider is gone.** There is no remount, no controlled landing, no continuation with the rider aboard.

### Why this ending is excluded from the default

The bull and the elephant both resolve their loop: separation is answered by reconnection. The crocodile substitutes spectacle for resolution — the rider leaves and never comes back. That is a different, weaker structure for this skill's purpose, because the payoff of the format is *the return*, not the height.

**Use the crocodile for:** eyewitness camera behavior · lateral boat/vehicle tracking · ramp timing · the separation instant · how far spectacle can be pushed · viral pacing at the 10-second length.
**Never use it for:** the ending. A breach, a splash-out, a vanishing rider or any equivalent finish is an override the user must explicitly ask for.

## What the two primaries agree on

Both primaries, filmed on different continents with different animals and different ramps, produce the same measured structure. That agreement is what makes it a formula rather than a one-off.

| Beat | Ref A (bull) | Ref B (elephant) | Share of runtime |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mounted hook | 0:00–0:01.5 | 0:00–0:01.5 | 0–15 % |
| Approach | 0:01.5–0:03.2 | 0:01.5–0:03.2 | 15–32 % |
| Ramp contact + climb | 0:03.2–0:03.9 | 0:03.2–0:04.1 | 32–40 % |
| Launch + separation | 0:03.9–0:04.2 | 0:04.1–0:04.4 | 39–44 % |
| Aerial trick | 0:04.2–0:07.2 | 0:04.4–0:07.2 | 42–72 % |
| Descent + realign | 0:07.2–0:08.3 | 0:07.2–0:07.6 | 72–80 % |
| Remount + absorb | 0:08.3–0:08.9 | 0:07.6–0:07.9 | 80–89 % |
| Landing + continuation | 0:08.9–0:10.0 | 0:07.9–0:10.0 | 89–100 % |

**They also agree on all of this:**

- The rider is **already mounted in frame one**. No establishing shot, ever.
- The ramp is **visible before it is used**, and belongs to the environment.
- The rider **actively extends upward** at the crest. He is not thrown.
- The animal performs its **own** rotation, at a speed set by its mass.
- The rider stays **directly above the animal**, one to two body-lengths clear, for the entire airborne phase.
- Contact is **feet-first onto the back**, followed immediately by a crouch that absorbs it.
- The animal's landing throws **environmental debris** — dust in both cases.
- The clip **ends shortly after the landing**, on forward motion, with no second action.
- The stunt travels **across the frame**, and its arc is legible because of that. Reference A shows this from a true side profile; even Reference B's rear-facing camera keeps the launch and landing broadside enough to read. The arc is the whole point — a camera that flattens it into depth destroys the format.

**Where they legitimately differ** — and this is the map for adapting to any new animal:

| | Ref A — bull | Ref B — elephant |
|---|---|---|
| Rider's approach posture | standing surf stance, arms out | low crouch, hands down |
| Camera position | lateral profile, moving alongside — **this is the standard** | behind, from a stationary vehicle — **do not copy this** |
| Ramp | improvised man-made (metal sheet over a shack) | natural environmental (dirt mound) |
| Approach gait | fast gallop | heavy run |
| Rotation speed | quick, snappy | slow, weighty |
| Landing | front hooves first, immediate gallop | rear feet first, huge dust burst |
| Foreground | clean | passengers' heads, roll bar, canopy edge |

Neither version is more correct **on the structure** — that is fixed. The **body mechanics, ramp, posture and environment** are what adapt to the animal.

> **The one exception is camera position, and it is not a free variable.** Reference A's lateral profile is the standard for every build. Reference B happens to be shot from behind a safari vehicle because that is where the person filming was sitting — it is an accident of that particular clip, not part of the format. Copying it produces footage where the animal runs away from the viewer and the stunt reads as depth rather than as an arc across the frame, which is precisely the failure mode this skill exists to avoid. Take Reference B's *body mechanics, ramp and dust*; take Reference A's *camera*. Full rule: `PROMPT_FORMAT.md` → **The camera orientation lock**.
