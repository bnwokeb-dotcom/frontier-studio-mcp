# Funny Animal Stunts — Stunt Logic

# Stunt Logic

The reusable formula, its twelve beats, and how to bend each one to a different animal without breaking it.

Derived from the measured agreement between Primary References A and B — see `REFERENCE_ANALYSIS.md`.

---

## The twelve beats

Every prompt contains all twelve, in this order, at both durations.

| # | Beat | What must be visible |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Mounted opening** | Rider already on the animal, in contact, moving forward, environment readable. Frame one. |
| 2 | **Moving approach** | Animal travelling at speed toward something. Rider adjusting posture, preparing. |
| 3 | **Natural ramp** | The launch structure enters frame *before* it is used. Environment-appropriate. |
| 4 | **Synchronized launch** | Animal contacts and climbs the ramp; rider compresses; both leave the lip together. |
| 5 | **Rider separation** | Rider extends upward off the animal's back and separates cleanly. One clear instant. |
| 6 | **Rider aerial rotation** | The named trick, with direction, body shape and rotation count. |
| 7 | **Animal airborne trajectory** | The animal's *own* rotation and arc, at a speed set by its mass. |
| 8 | **Synchronized descent** | Both fall on related arcs. The animal drifts under the rider, slightly ahead. |
| 9 | **Rider re-alignment** | Rider opens from the tuck, finds the animal, matches its direction, extends feet down. |
| 10 | **Rider remount** | Feet-first contact on the animal's back. Knees bend. Rider absorbs and grips. |
| 11 | **Animal landing** | Touchdown in the animal's own correct order, with an environmental burst. |
| 12 | **Continuation** | Forward motion resumes with the rider aboard. End shortly after. |

Beats 1–2 are the hook. Beats 6–7 get the most detailed writing. Beat 10 always gets **its own timestamp segment** — never a clause tacked onto beat 11.

## Beat 3 — the environment chooses the ramp

The environment is not a backdrop. It supplies the launch structure, and changing the environment changes the ramp.

| Environment | Ramp |
|---|---|
| Savanna | dirt mound · dry embankment · raised dusty trail · termite mound |
| Ranch / field | dirt ramp · raised earth berm · natural mound · a metal sheet over rubble |
| Jungle | muddy embankment · raised root-covered terrain · dirt incline · fallen buttress trunk |
| Arctic | snow ridge · ice-covered mound · compacted snow bank · wind-carved drift |
| Desert | sand dune · rocky incline · raised desert ridge |
| Mountain | rocky rise · gravel embankment · shale slope |
| Riverbank / swamp | mud bank · silt shelf · washed-out earth lip |

Both primary references show both kinds: Reference B uses a **natural** mound, Reference A uses an **improvised man-made** sheet over a collapsed shack. Either is valid. What matters is that it belongs to the place.

**Rules for the ramp:**

- It is **visible in frame before it is used** — at least a second of approach at 10 s, longer at 15 s.
- The animal **visibly contacts and travels up it**. Never cut from ground to air.
- Its angle is readable, roughly 25–35°. The viewer must understand where the upward momentum came from.
- It never appears from nowhere, and it never changes shape between the approach and the launch.

## Beats 6–7 — two trajectories, one shared arc

Treat the stunt as two synchronized paths that must make spatial sense against each other in every frame.

**Trajectory A — the animal**
approaches the launch point → contacts and climbs the incline → launches → follows one continuous airborne arc → performs an animal-appropriate aerial rotation → begins descending → arrives in a position that lets the rider reconnect → lands → continues forward.

**Trajectory B — the rider**
begins physically connected → prepares for the launch → stays attached through the initial takeoff → separates upward on momentum plus his own extension → performs the chosen rotation → stays spatially related to the animal → completes the rotation above it → descends toward it → reconnects → stabilizes after impact.

### The station-keeping rule

For the entire airborne phase, the rider stays **directly above the animal with one to two body-lengths of clear air between them.** Not overlapping. Not drifting out of relationship. This is measurable in every frame of both primary references, and it is the single thing that makes the remount legible when it arrives.

### Write the air, don't summarize it

The airborne phase gets the most detailed choreography in the prompt. Describe, in order:

1. the animal's orientation at takeoff
2. the rider's connection at that instant
3. the exact moment of separation
4. the rider's rotation direction
5. the rider's body position through the rotation
6. the animal's own body rotation
7. the vertical distance between them
8. the horizontal offset between them
9. the apex of the jump
10. the rider completing the rotation
11. the animal beginning its descent
12. the rider realigning above the animal
13. the reconnection trajectory

❌ "They soar through the air and perform an incredible flip."
✅ "The bull leaves the lip nose-up and rolls backward onto its spine, all four legs pointing skyward at the apex. The rider, roughly two body-lengths above and slightly behind it, pulls into a tight tuck and completes one and a half backward rotations, staying framed directly over the animal the whole way."

## Beat 10 — the remount, in full

This is the beat prompts get wrong, so it is specified rather than described.

**Never write only:** "The rider lands back on the animal."

**Write the process:**

> The rider finishes the backward rotation above the animal, opens out of the tuck, and extends his legs downward. He locates the animal beneath him and squares his torso and hips to its direction of travel. The animal stays underneath and slightly ahead, giving him a believable interception path. He descends onto its back just behind the shoulders; his feet make contact first, his knees bend deeply to absorb the impact, his hands drop to the animal's neck, and he settles into a low crouch as the animal completes its own landing.

**Non-negotiables in this beat:**

- Contact is **on top of the animal**, never beside, behind or in front of it.
- Contact is **feet-first**, then absorbed by bending — never a rigid arrival.
- The animal beneath is **visibly the same individual** that launched.
- The rider is **visibly the same person** who left.
- The remount holds **its own timestamp range**. It is not folded into the landing beat.

**Adapt the contact point to the animal:**

| Animal | Lands on | Grips |
|---|---|---|
| Elephant | broad back just behind the head | the base of the neck / ears |
| Bull, buffalo | back just behind the shoulder hump | the hump / withers |
| Horse, zebra, camel | the back between withers and mid-spine | mane / neck |
| Rhino, giraffe | high shoulder ridge | neck ridge |
| Bear, polar bear, gorilla, lion, tiger | the broad shoulders and upper back | the loose scruff / shoulder fur |
| Crocodile | flat mid-back behind the forelimbs | the ridged spine |

## Adapting to a new animal

**Preserve the structure. Rewrite the mechanics.** Never force an elephant to move like a bull, or a horse to rotate like an elephant.

Adapt: stride · jump mechanics · limb position · body rotation · weight · landing order · momentum.

| Animal | Approach gait | Launch | Air | Landing | Rotation speed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Elephant** | heavy pounding run, dust at every step | drives up with the hind legs, slow and powerful | slow full backward somersault, trunk and legs swinging outward | rear feet first, front legs extended, huge dust burst | slow, heavy |
| **Bull / buffalo** | fast gallop, head low | snaps off the lip nose-up, front legs folding | quick, tight backward somersault, legs tucked | front hooves first, immediately back into a gallop | quick, snappy |
| **Horse / zebra** | extended gallop, long stride | powerful hindquarter drive, front legs reaching | long arcing backward rotation, legs extended | front hooves then hind, canters on | medium-fast, elegant |
| **Rhino** | low battering charge | short heavy launch, minimal air | slow half-to-full rotation, body stiff | all four at once, heavy earth impact | very slow |
| **Camel** | loping pace, bouncing gait | ungainly upward lurch, long legs splayed | loose, gangly backward rotation | fore then hind, sand spray, resumes loping | medium, loose |
| **Giraffe** | slow-motion rocking gallop | enormous slow launch, neck swinging back | very slow rotation, neck arcing through the turn | fore then hind, long legs absorbing, huge stride out | very slow, sweeping |
| **Bear / polar bear** | rolling bounding lope, shoulders rocking | explosive bound, forepaws reaching | compact backward roll, paws tucked to the chest | all four bunched, snow spray, immediate lope | medium, compact |
| **Lion / tiger** | fluid sprint, spine flexing | cat-like spring, back arched | fast athletic backward rotation, tail counter-rotating | forepaws first, flowing straight back into a sprint | fast, athletic |
| **Gorilla** | knuckle-run, shoulders leading | powerful two-arm push off the lip | tucked backward roll, arms wrapped in | knuckles and feet, rocking forward | fast, compact |
| **Crocodile** | low fast body-swinging run | shoves up on the forelimbs, tail whipping | slow rotation on the long axis, tail trailing | belly-flat slam, mud or water burst | slow, rigid |

**Any animal not in the table:** derive it from mass and limb structure. Heavy → slower rotation, longer climb, bigger landing burst. Light and athletic → faster rotation, more air, quicker recovery. Long-necked or long-tailed → that appendage swings through the rotation and shows the turn.

**Small animals scaled up:** keep species proportions, markings and limb count exact; only the size changes. A scaled-up animal rotates like a heavy animal, not like a small one.

## Beat 12 — the ending

The animal lands in its own correct order and the environment reacts: dust burst · dirt displacement · snow spray · sand · leaves and debris · a small camera shake at the impact.

The rider stays mounted. The animal may continue running forward; the rider stabilizes; the camera may react slightly. **End shortly after the successful completion.** Never add a second, unrelated action after the landing.

## Failure modes to design against

| Failure | Prevented by |
|---|---|
| Rider lands beside the animal | Beat 10 written in full, with the interception path stated |
| Rider never comes back | The station-keeping rule through the whole airborne phase |
| A second animal appears for the landing | Naming the animal as the same individual in the continuity rules |
| Ramp appears from nowhere | Beat 3's visible approach requirement |
| Stunt truncated at 10 seconds | The proportional timing tables in `PROMPT_FORMAT.md` |
| Rider identity drifts mid-air | The reference image cited as the identity source, restated in the continuity rules |
| Animal morphs during the rotation | The animal's rotation written as one continuous body movement |
| Flip is vague and unreadable | The thirteen-point airborne checklist above |
