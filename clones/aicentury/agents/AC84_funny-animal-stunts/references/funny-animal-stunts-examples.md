# Funny Animal Stunts — Examples

# Examples

Four complete builds. Same stunt grammar every time — **ride → launch → aerial trick → return → remount → landing** — with the body mechanics, ramp and camera adapted to the animal and the environment.

The character is never described from imagination. Every example defers identity to the user's uploaded reference image, exactly as the skill does in a real build.

## Example A — Elephant · African savanna · 10 seconds

*Heavy animal, natural ramp, lateral tracking camera. Body mechanics from Primary Reference B; camera from Primary Reference A.*

# VIDEO PROMPT

```
Create photorealistic viral smartphone footage captured in one continuous handheld shot. The uploaded reference character is riding an adult African elephant across open savanna, moving from left to right across frame. Preserve the character's exact identity, face, build, hairstyle, clothing, footwear, and distinctive visual features from the uploaded reference image. The elephant is full-grown, with deeply creased grey hide, large fanned ears, curved ivory tusks, and a heavy swinging trunk. The surrounding environment consists of pale gold dry grass, scattered flat-topped acacia trees running to the horizon, a worn dirt trail, loose dust that lifts at every footfall, and flat overcast light.

The camera films the entire stunt from a strict lateral side profile, positioned roughly 90 degrees perpendicular to the elephant's direction of travel. The elephant's full flank stays visible throughout - head toward the right of frame, tail toward the left - and both rider and elephant travel horizontally across the frame, never toward or away from the lens. The camera tracks alongside them like someone running parallel to the stunt and filming it from the side. It may pan, follow, tilt, shake and adjust speed to hold the framing, but the viewing angle never rotates into a head-on, frontal, rear, three-quarter, diagonal or orbiting perspective at any point - including during the launch, the airborne phase, the descent and the landing.

TIMELINE

0:00–0:01.5
The rider is already crouched low on the elephant's back just behind its head, both hands pressed to the base of its neck, knees bent, body tilted forward with the animal's motion. The elephant is running across open savanna from left to right, held in full side profile, ears swinging, dust bursting from each footfall.

0:01.5–0:03.0
The elephant builds speed on a worn dirt trail. A large conical mound of loose dry earth comes into view ahead and slightly right, roughly as tall as the elephant, steep-sided, the top scuffed bare. The rider shifts his weight forward and drops lower over the neck.

0:03.0–0:04.0
The elephant runs straight at the near face of the mound and climbs it, hind legs driving hard, dust sheeting off its flanks. The climb is slow and heavy. At the crest the rider rises out of the crouch and extends upward.

0:04.0–0:07.0
The elephant launches nose-up off the top of the mound. In the same instant the rider pushes off its back and separates cleanly, rising above and slightly behind it. The elephant begins a slow full backward somersault — pitching up, rolling onto its back with all four legs and its trunk swinging outward, continuing round. The rider pulls into a tight tuck, knees to chest, and rotates backward faster than the animal, holding station roughly two body-lengths directly above it with clear sky between them. At the apex the elephant is fully inverted, legs skyward, and the rider is directly over it, still rotating.

0:07.0–0:08.5
The elephant comes out of its rotation feet-down and begins descending toward the ground. The rider opens out of the tuck, extends his legs downward, locates the elephant beneath him and squares his torso and hips to its direction of travel. The elephant stays underneath and slightly ahead, giving him a clear interception path.

0:08.5–0:10.0
The rider descends onto the elephant's broad back just behind its head. His feet make contact first, his knees bend deeply to absorb the impact, his hands drop to the base of the neck, and he settles into a low crouch. In the same beat the elephant lands rear feet first with its front legs extended, throwing a massive dust burst that rolls up around both of them. It runs forward through the dust with the rider crouched and mounted, and the shot ends there.

CAMERA BEHAVIOR

Handheld smartphone filmed from a safari vehicle driving parallel to the elephant, holding station alongside it at roughly its speed so the animal stays broadside to the lens. The vehicle's window frame and another passenger's shoulder cross the lower corner of frame and occasionally clip the action. The camera pans to keep the elephant centred, tilts up hard at the launch and holds wide through the airborne phase with the elephant low and the rider high, then tilts back down for the landing and takes a small shake at the impact. Imperfect tracking, natural motion blur, realistic phone exposure against the bright overcast sky. The operator reacts a beat late to everything. The camera stays level with the elephant's travel line and never swings behind it, in front of it, or around it.

CONTINUITY & MOTION RULES

- Strict side-profile throughout; the camera stays perpendicular to the elephant's direction of travel.
- The elephant's full flank stays visible; it never turns toward or away from the lens.
- No head-on, frontal, rear, three-quarter, diagonal or orbiting angles at any point.
- The camera may track, pan, tilt and shake, but the viewing angle never rotates.
- The rider is the same person throughout, matching the uploaded reference exactly.
- The elephant is the same individual from start to finish — same tusks, ears, hide and proportions.
- No second elephant and no second rider at any point.
- The rider lands on top of the elephant, not beside or behind it.
- Continuous forward momentum; consistent gravity; one unbroken rotation each.
- No teleportation, no pose reset, no size change, no morphing during the rotation.
- One continuous shot, no cuts.
- No captions, subtitles or on-screen text. No background music.
```
