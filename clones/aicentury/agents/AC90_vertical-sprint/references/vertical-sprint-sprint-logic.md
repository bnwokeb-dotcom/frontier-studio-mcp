# Vertical Sprint — Sprint Logic

# Sprint Logic

The reusable formula, its beats, and how to vary the character, structure, crowd and trick without breaking the format. Derived from `REFERENCE_ANALYSIS.md`.

---

## The nine beats

Every prompt contains all nine, in this order.

| # | Beat | What must be visible | 15 s default |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Start-line hook** | Athlete close to the lens, back turned (or entering past it). Structure dead centre at the end of the runway. Crowd behind barriers on both sides, phones up. | 0.0–1.5 |
| 2 | **Sprint away** | Explosive acceleration straight down the runway, away from the lens. The athlete shrinks. The crowd reacts as they pass. | 1.5–4.5 |
| 3 | **Transition + vertical ascent** | Feet hit the curved base at full speed. The body rotates with the curve until it is perpendicular to the face. Sprinting straight up. The camera tilts up and zooms in. | 4.5–7.5 |
| 4 | **Top edge / launch** | The athlete reaches the lip, plants, and pops upward off the top edge into open sky. | 7.5–8.5 |
| 5 | **The trick (peak)** | One named trick, high above the edge, edge kept in the lower frame. | 8.5–10.5 |
| 6 | **Landing** | Feet-first on the top deck or lip, knees absorb, a brief balanced beat. | 10.5–11.5 |
| 7 | **Descent run** | Turns, runs straight down the vertical face facing the camera, out of the curve onto the runway. The camera tilts down and zooms out. | 11.5–13.5 |
| 8 | **Return to the lens** | Sprints back up the runway toward the camera, growing in frame. The crowd erupts. | (inside 11.5–13.5 / 13.5–15.0) |
| 9 | **Celebration close-up** | Stops close to the lens, chest to head in frame: arms up, roar, pointing to the sky, a flex or a pose. The crowd is cheering behind. End. | 13.5–15.0 |

Beats 1 and 5 decide whether the video works. Beat 1 needs instant scale. Beat 5 needs a readable silhouette against the sky. Beat 9 is the emotional payoff and the loop point.

## Structures

### The runnable-wall test (every option must pass)

1. **Flat runway in.** A straight, open approach of at least 30–60 m, lined by barriers.
2. **A curved transition at the base.** A quarter-pipe curve, a flared hull or a bolted-on steel/wood launch ramp, so the run can bend from horizontal to vertical. If the real structure has no curve, **add one** and say so.
3. **One continuous, smooth, unbroken face.** No windows to trip on, no gaps, no overhang. It is **a true 90° vertical face** (never a slope or a flare), **50–60 times the athlete's height (about 90–110 m)**. See **Scale** below.
4. **A top edge or deck to land on**, with sky above it so the trick reads.
5. **Space for a crowd** behind barriers along the runway.
6. **Visually believable.** Real materials, real location. The *run* can defy physics, but the place must not.

### Scale: what makes it spectacular

The height and the steepness are the spectacle. Two lessons from the live tests (Wan 3.0, 2026-10-02):
- A tower written as "fifteen times his height" came out short and squat. "Thirty times" was better, but still not jaw-dropping.
- The ship bow was tall but wide and flaring, so it read as a big slope, not a cliff. **Width and flare kill steepness.**

State all five in every prompt:

1. **Runway:** a long flat runway of about 80–100 m (roughly 50 times the athlete's height) runs from the camera to the base. The ramp never starts at the camera.
2. **Height:** the face is **50–60 times the athlete's height**, as tall as a **30-storey skyscraper**. It towers far above everything around it (stands, cranes, buildings, trees).
3. **Steepness:** a short, tight curve at the very bottom (at most about a tenth of the height), then a **perfectly vertical 90° face, a sheer cliff**, all the way to the top. Never a slope, flare, bulge or lean-back.
4. **Slenderness:** the face is **narrow and tall, at least 6 times taller than it is wide**, like a needle or a monolith. Prefer naturally sheer, slender structures (towers, pylons, chimneys, monoliths, skyscraper faces, single silos) over wide or rounded ones (hulls, dams, stadium bowls). Use wide ones only if the user picks them, and then describe only a narrow vertical strip as the running lane.
5. **In frame:** in frame one, the structure rises out of the top of the frame; its top is cut off or only just visible, so the camera has to tilt up to find it. At the top the athlete is a speck (1–2 % of the frame height) against the sky, and the climb takes at least 3 seconds of continuous running. The camera's zoom keeps the speck readable.

Useful phrases: "as tall as a thirty-storey skyscraper", "a sheer 90-degree cliff of plywood", "a needle-thin tower", "the crowd at its base looks like ants", "its top almost touches the low clouds".

### Structure → environment details to write

Always name: the **surface material and colour**, the **height** (relative to the athlete, e.g. "about fifty-five times the athlete's height, a thirty-storey sheer vertical face"), the **curve at the base**, the **top deck/edge** (railing, flags, staff silhouettes), **what is behind it** (sky, cranes, skyline, stadium tiers), and the **runway** (material, length, barriers).

## Crowd and event atmosphere

Default, adapted to the location:

- Steel crowd-control barricades line **both sides** of a straight runway that runs from the camera to the base of the structure.
- A dense crowd 3–8 rows deep behind the barriers, most of them holding phones up filming. In stadium settings, full tiers rise behind.
- A few staff, photographers or flags on the top deck.
- Reactions: cheering as the athlete passes, gasps and hands on heads at the peak, a roar and applause at the return.
- Optional foreground realism: the camera owner's hand and phone, or the back of a spectator's head, along the **lower edge only**, never covering the runway or the structure.
- Event flavour: an extreme-sports championship, a festival stunt show, a stadium halftime stunt, a farm-show challenge, a city street event. **Never name a sponsor or brand.**

Keep the crowd description to two sentences in the prompt. The crowd is context, not the subject.

## Tricks

### The trick menu (STEP 4)

| # | Trick | How to write it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 🔄 **Backflip** (signature) | Pops off the lip, tucks knees to chest, one clean backward rotation 2–3 body heights above the edge, opens out feet-down. |
| 2 | 🕊️ **Swan layout** | Stands at the lip with arms spread wide for a beat, then a fully stretched layout backflip, body straight like a plank, arms out. |
| 3 | 🤸 **Handstand → backflip** | Plants both hands on the top edge, holds a vertical handstand for a beat, snaps down to the feet and launches straight into a tuck backflip. |
| 4 | 🌀 **Twisting flip** | A backflip with one full twist around the long axis, arms pulled to the chest during the twist. |
| 5 | 🔁 **Double backflip** | Higher launch, two tight consecutive tucked backward rotations, opens out cleanly. |
| 6 | 🎲 **Pick for me** | Choose using the table below. |
| 7 | ➕ **More** | Show 5 from the More pool, numbered, with **More** again. |

### More pool (draw 5)

🔥 Air combo: 2–3 connected flips/twists in one long airtime · Front flip off the top · gainer (backflip travelling forward) · side flip (aerial) · butterfly twist (martial arts) · 540 tornado kick in the air · cork / corkscrew (off-axis twisting flip) · double layout · triple tuck (30 s only) · front flip **on the way down** the face (clips 05, 06, 17) · backflip, land, then a second flip down the face · straddle pike jump into a backflip · aerial cartwheel along the top edge · "superman" stretch at the peak before tucking · split leap at the peak · freerunning "kong" vault over the top railing into a flip

### Auto-pick (🎲)

| Character | Pick |
|---|---|
| Bodybuilder / strongman | big tuck backflip (the heavy-body contrast is the spectacle), then a flex at the finish |
| Gymnast | handstand → backflip, or a layout with a full twist |
| Martial artist / kung fu outfit | backflip at the top, a front flip on the way down, a martial-arts salute at the finish (clip 17) |
| Freerunner / parkour | gainer or cork, then a running celebration |
| Casual "ordinary" person | swan pose → tuck backflip (clips 04, 12) |
| Dancer | split leap at the peak → backflip |
| Older person | one clean tuck backflip, then a joyful arms-up finish |
| Uploaded reference, unknown style | the signature tuck backflip |

### Rules for writing any trick

- **One trick at the peak by default.** Exceptions: a second flip on the way down (the More option), the **air combo** below, or the 30 s extended version.
- **Air combo (15 s):** 2–3 connected rotations in one long airtime (about 3 s, e.g. 8.0–11.0s, which shortens the descent beat). Name each rotation in order (e.g. tuck backflip → layout with a full twist → tuck backflip), launch extra high (4–5 body heights), keep everything on one backward axis, and land feet-first on the top edge.
- Always state **direction** (backward/forward/sideways), **body shape** (tuck/pike/layout), **rotation count**, **height** ("about three body heights above the top edge") and **how it opens out** (feet-down, toward the deck).
- Break it into stages across the timeline: *pop → rotate → apex → open out → land*. Never write "does an amazing flip".
- Keep it simple enough for a video model: no chains of more than two elements, and no mid-air direction changes.

## Generated characters

Used when the user picks **B2 · Create one for me** in STEP 2. (With **B1 · I'll describe them myself**, use their description and fill only the gaps.)

### The rule: a new person every time

The characters in this skill's files (the tweed-suit bowl-cut man, the redhead in the mustard top, the woman in cream sweatpants, the kung-fu athlete, the flag-singlet sprinters) are **examples only**. **Never output them**, and never output a near-copy. Each run builds a fresh character by combining the dimensions below, so two runs almost never produce the same person.

### The generator: pick one from each row, independently

| Dimension | Range (mix freely; these are directions, not a closed list) |
|---|---|
| **Vibe / archetype** | power athlete · casual ordinary person · bodybuilder · martial artist · gymnast · freerunner · dancer · office worker · retiree · student · chef · delivery rider · festival-goer · eccentric dandy · farmer · nurse · street-fashion kid · rock-band look · hiker · surfer · anything else that makes a strong silhouette |
| **Age** | anywhere from 18 to 70; vary it (not always 20s) |
| **Build** | wiry · lanky · stocky · broad and heavily muscled · athletic medium · petite · tall and thin · soft and round (in plain words) |
| **Skin tone & look** | vary freely across all skin tones and heritages; describe the visible look, not a label |
| **Hair** | something that moves or reads at distance: braid, afro, mohawk, mullet, long loose curls, high ponytail, buzz cut with dyed tips, silver bun, bowl cut, top knot… |
| **Outfit** | one coherent outfit that fits the vibe. It may be absurd (business suit, chef whites, wedding dress, pyjamas) or sporty |
| **Signature colour** | **one saturated colour block** that contrasts with the structure and sky (neon orange, cobalt, lime, hot pink, mustard, red, flag print…) |
| **One memorable detail** | e.g. round sunglasses, a big moustache, knee-high socks, a headband, a bow tie, a gold chain, a cape |

### Checks before showing the character

- **Different from the previous character in this session** in at least vibe, age band and colour.
- Never ultra-muscular two runs in a row, and not as the default.
- **Footwear is always visible and runnable** (sneakers, trainers, boots, slippers). Bare feet look wrong to a video model.
- Build is described in plain words.
- Show it to the user in **one or two lines**, e.g. *"Your athlete: a stocky 58-year-old with a white handlebar moustache, cobalt-blue chef's whites and red clogs."* Offer **another** for a re-roll.
- In the prompt's `CHARACTER` section, write the full description once, then use one short tag throughout ("the chef in cobalt whites").

### Reference archetypes from the viral clips (inspiration, not templates)

Patriotic power athlete in a flag singlet (clips 07–16) · casual ordinary person in baggy sweatpants (03–06) · giant bodybuilder in neon shorts (01–02) · martial artist in a kung-fu outfit (17). Use these to understand *why* they work (bold colour, clear silhouette, ordinary-vs-superhuman contrast), not as outfits to copy.

## Uploaded references

- Read **face, hair, skin tone, build, outfit, colours, footwear, accessories** straight off the image or character sheet.
- In `CHARACTER`, write the identity lock (wording in `PROMPT_FORMAT.md`) plus a **one-line visual anchor** of what you see ("bald, heavily muscled man in neon-orange shorts and black trainers"). The anchor helps the model keep tracking them when they are tiny on the wall.
- **Never change the outfit** for sport. If they are in jeans, they sprint in jeans. The contrast is part of the appeal.
- If the image is unclear (cropped body, several people), ask one short clarifying question.

## Physics: coherent, not realistic

The stunt is impossible. **Never remove the spectacle.** What must hold is internal coherence:

| Phase | Write |
|---|---|
| Acceleration | Low drive phase, arms pumping, feet striking the runway hard, speed visibly building. |
| Transition | Feet stay planted on the curve. The body tilts progressively with the surface, so the torso is always perpendicular to the surface underfoot. |
| Vertical run | Short, fast, flat-footed steps on the face, as if gravity has rotated to follow the wall. Steady speed, no sliding, no climbing with the hands. |
| Launch | A last strong step at the lip, arms swing up, the body leaves the edge travelling upward. |
| Trick | One continuous rotation on one axis, with a clear apex. Gravity pulls back down toward the deck. |
| Landing | Feet first, knees bend, arms out for balance, a visible weight transfer. |
| Descent | Turns to face the camera and runs straight down the face with the same wall-gravity, then out of the curve onto the runway. |
| Finish | Decelerates over several strides; no instant stop. |

Never: teleporting, floating, hovering, sudden direction changes, scale changes, extra or missing limbs, body warping, a second athlete, hands used to climb, sliding down the face, or landing somewhere new.
