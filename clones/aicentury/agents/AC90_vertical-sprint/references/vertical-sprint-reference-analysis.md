# Reference Analysis

Seventeen reference clips sit in `GPT Maker/vertical sprint/`. Every observation below comes from frames extracted at 1 fps for all 17 clips and at 2 fps for two calibration clips (#12, #16). Scene-change detection (threshold 0.3) was run on every file. Nothing is inferred from thumbnails or titles.

> **About view counts.** The files carry no view data (no metadata tags, no counts in filenames). The user reports that most clips passed 1M views and some reached tens of millions (~77M). Per-clip view comparisons therefore **cannot** be made from the files. The weighting below uses the signals that *are* measurable: how often each pattern recurs, and how often one creator iterated on it (the `sksurajinfluncer` series is 10 of the 17 clips, which suggests that exact variant kept performing). If view counts are supplied later, rerun the comparison and update the defaults.

## Camera — dedicated analysis

| Property | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Operator** | A spectator with a smartphone. In about half the clips a hand, phone or the back of a head is visible in the lower frame. |
| **Position** | **In the crowd: front row, behind the steel barrier at the start of the runway, just beside it, facing the structure along the runway.** The barrier rail (07, 10, 12, 13), spectators' heads and shoulders (05, 06, 09, 16) or raised phones and hands (01, 02, 04, 14, 15) are in the foreground. It is never a camera operator standing on the runway behind the athlete. **Never moves** during the clip (13 of 17 strictly; 4 start slightly off-axis and swing to follow, then hold). |
| **Height** | Standing eye level, ~1.5–1.7 m. Never a drone, never ground-level. |
| **Angle** | Straight-on rear view of the athlete running away, the reverse for the return. Effectively **0° to the runway axis**, never side-on, never a three-quarter tracking angle. Becomes a strong **low-angle upward view** during the trick. |
| **Lens feel** | Phone main lens (~26 mm equivalent) at the start and end, plus **2–5× punch-in zoom** for the top and the trick. Compression from the zoom makes the structure look enormous. |
| **Movement** | Pan: minimal. **Tilt: up for the ascent, down for the return.** **Zoom: in for the ascent, out for the return.** Dolly/track: none. Orbit: none. Natural handheld micro-shake throughout. |
| **During the trick** | Held steady with a slight reactive drift. Top edge in the lower 25–40 % of the frame, athlete small but crisp against open sky, 1.5–4 body heights above the edge. |
| **Landing** | Always in frame. |
| **Environment visible** | First ~4 s: runway, both crowd walls, the whole structure. Middle (ascent and trick): structure face, then top edge + sky only, with no crowd. Last ~4 s: runway, crowd, structure base again. |

**Most common camera setup and path (the default):**
> Spectator in the front row behind the start-line barrier, beside the runway, phone at eye level, foreground framed by the barrier rail and other spectators' heads and phones → wide shot with the athlete in the foreground, back turned → static while the athlete shrinks into the distance → tilts up and zooms in as they climb → holds on the top edge and sky for the trick → tilts down and zooms out as they run back → ends on a medium close-up of the athlete celebrating, crowd behind.

**Variants seen:**
- **B. Side entry (03, 04, 10, 13):** the athlete runs past the camera from beside it, and the camera swings to follow them onto the axis. After that it behaves exactly like the default.
- **C. Mid-climb start (05, 06, 17):** shot from behind the spectators' heads, with the athlete already at or on the ramp. The loop is shorter and there is more time for trick variety, e.g. extra flips on the way down.

## Composition of the first frame (the hook)

In 15 of 17 clips, frame one already contains everything needed to understand the video:

1. **The structure, dead centre**, at the end of a straight runway, towering out of the top of the frame or close to it.
2. **Two walls of spectators** behind steel barriers converging toward it (one-point perspective).
3. **The athlete close to the lens with their back turned** (or about to run past it), so the scale contrast between person and wall is instant.
4. **Phones raised** in the crowd and often in the foreground.

Nobody needs to watch two seconds to know what is about to happen. That is the hook.

| Organisation | Clearly an organised event: barriers, staff on the top deck, flags, PA towers. Never a spontaneous street moment. |
| Branding | Event flags and banners are present but small. The prompt should evoke the event without naming sponsors. |

## Physics and realism

| | Finding |
|---|---|
| Environment | Fully photoreal and believable. Real materials, real light, wet ground, shadows. |
| The run | **Impossible but coherent.** The athlete runs straight up a true-vertical face at full sprint, body perpendicular to the surface as if gravity followed the wall, with short, fast, flat-footed steps. |
| Hang time | Exaggerated: 1.5–2.5 s in the 15 s clips, ~10 s of repeated flips in the 30 s clips. |
| Rotation | Clean, readable, one axis. Tuck or layout backflips dominate. |
| Return | Runs straight down the vertical face facing the camera, out of the curve, onto the runway. |
| Ending | No crash, no fall. Always a triumphant, emotional finish near the lens. |

## Why it works: the viral mechanics

1. **Instant scale contrast.** A tiny human, a giant wall, both readable in frame one.
2. **One axis of motion.** Away, up, back. Nothing to decode, so it reads perfectly at phone size with no sound.
3. **Impossible action, believable footage.** A shaky spectator phone, real crowds and real weather make the physics feel "caught on camera".
4. **The peak is isolated against clean sky.** The flip silhouette is the thumbnail.
5. **A closed loop with a human payoff.** The athlete comes back to the lens and celebrates, and the last frame mirrors the first. That makes it rewatchable and loop-friendly.
6. **The crowd is social proof.** Hundreds of phones say "this is a real event worth filming".
7. **A strong single-colour outfit** (orange shorts, flag print, royal blue) keeps the athlete trackable when they are 2 % of the frame.
8. **Ordinary-person contrast** (casual sweatpants, sweaters) in 4 clips: "a normal person did *that*".

These eight points are the non-negotiable core of every prompt the skill writes.
